What is Damla?

Damla is dollar-cost averaging (DCA) for Stellar: you commit to buying a fixed amount of XLM with USDC at a cadence you type yourself (every 6 hours, every 3 days, every 2 weeks, anything from an hour to 90 days), for 2 to 18 purchases, starting now or on a date you pick. You approve once in Freighter, and the purchases execute automatically on Stellar's built-in DEX. It is non-custodial: your USDC stays in your own wallet until each swap settles. It uses no smart contract.

Damla is Turkish for "drop", from the proverb damlaya damlaya göl olur: drop by drop, a lake is formed. That is the whole product.

Architecture

Damla is built on pre-authorized transaction chains using classic Stellar features (CAP-21 preconditions, Protocol 19). The channel signs each transaction; one wallet-signed setup authorizes their exact hashes. No Soroban or contract account is needed.

1

One approval for exact future transaction hashes

Damla builds one Stellar transaction for each scheduled purchase. Every transaction contains one PathPaymentStrictSend operation: a fixed amount of your USDC to XLM, with a destMin floor. Before wallet approval, the browser decodes all transactions and compares their hashes, payment terms and floor against a direct Horizon quote. One Freighter approval adds those exact hashes as pre-authorized signers to your account. Validators accept only matching hashes; the amount, asset, destination, floor and time window cannot be altered. The wallet prompt itself shows signer hashes, while the browser confirmation shows the decoded terms.

2

A fresh channel account per plan

Each plan gets its own brand-new channel account. It is the source of every purchase transaction (it pays the fees and owns the sequence clock), while the swap operation's source is you. Your later wallet activity cannot change the channel sequence. The channel never holds your USDC. A pre-authorized cleanup transaction removes unused purchase signers and, when a treasury is configured, merges the channel back to that treasury.

The service clears the channel's secret key from its active database row when your plan is activated. It signs your purchases once, then the running service no longer uses it. Historical database pages and backups may retain older copies, so protecting those files is part of the security model.

3

The protocol keeps time, not our server

Purchase i carries time bounds for its calendar window (it opens at t0 + (i-1)·P and closes two periods later) and a minSeqAge of one full period: it is invalid by consensus until the channel's sequence number has been untouched for a whole period. So a purchase can never run before its date, and two purchases can never land closer together than one period, no matter what our server does. Submitting early is not "against the rules": every validator simply rejects it with tx_too_early.

4

Skipped purchases don't break the chain

Every transaction carries minSeqNum = the channel's starting sequence, so purchase i is valid for any channel sequence between the start and its own number, not for a single point. If purchase 3 is skipped (window closed, XLM above your ceiling, no USDC), purchase 4 still works. Executing any purchase advances the sequence and invalidates all earlier envelopes for good. A swap that fails at the operation level consumes its slot and the chain continues.

5

The trigger service

A small service stores the channel-signed, hash-authorized envelopes and submits each one when its window opens. Before submitting it checks the market floor and your USDC balance, because a failed on-chain transaction burns its slot. It never holds your wallet key; when configured, it holds a separate treasury key for channel funding and fees. You can export the envelopes and submit them yourself if the service disappears.

Security model

There is no contract to audit because there is no contract. The trust assumptions reduce to Stellar's own protocol rules. Even a fully compromised trigger service:

  • cannot submit a purchase early: time bounds and minSeqAge are checked by consensus
  • cannot run two purchases inside one period: minSeqAge again
  • cannot change the amount, asset, destination or floor: each exact hash is pre-authorized by the wallet setup (tx_bad_auth otherwise)
  • cannot touch anything else in your account: each pre-authorized purchase hash covers exactly one swap operation
  • cannot create new channel transactions from the active database after activation; backup key exposure remains a residual risk
  • can at worst fail to submit, or pick a bad moment inside a window. No purchase happens, or you get a fill no worse than your floor.

The protocol checks were exercised on testnet: four consecutive purchases show rejected early and tampered submissions, while the deliberate-skip run shows later purchases succeeding after a missed window, with on-chain hashes.

Cancelling a plan

Your USDC never leaves your wallet in advance. Stop tells the service to submit the pre-authorized cleanup, removing unused signers and releasing their temporary XLM reserve. If the service is unavailable, remove those signers yourself in Freighter or Stellar Lab. An already exported purchase envelope remains usable until its signer is removed or its window closes.

The floor (destMin)

Because transaction hashes are authorized weeks in advance, the minimum amount of XLM you accept per purchase is fixed at setup time. Damla does not treat this as a slippage setting, because a DCA buyer wants to keep buying as the price moves. It is a ceiling: you choose how far above today's price you are still willing to buy (+10%, +25%, +50%). The floor is today's quote divided by that factor.

If XLM rises past your ceiling, that purchase waits and, if the market does not come back within its two-period window, is skipped. Falling prices never block a purchase; you simply receive more XLM. The floor is also the hard bound on how badly any submission can fill, honest service or not.

Open design question, still: a floor fixed for 12 weeks is crude. A monthly re-sign cadence ("sign once a month, buy every week") is the likely next step. It changes nothing in the protocol layer, only how often the planner asks for signatures.

Roadmap

  • NowPlanner, Freighter signing, trigger service, plan page, export. Testnet, with a 1-minute demo cadence and a testnet USDC helper.
  • NextMainnet switch-on (a funded treasury for channel reserves), monthly re-sign option, e-mail or Telegram receipts.
  • LaterMore pairs (EURC, yXLM), higher purchase counts through wallet-side batch signing if wallets support it, a self-hosted trigger you can run yourself.
